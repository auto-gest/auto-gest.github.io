Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 6, 2026

This policy explains how the Android app AutoGest handles your information.

The short version
  • The app works fully offline. It does not request the Internet permission, so it cannot send anything to us or to anyone else.
  • We have no servers, no accounts, no advertising and no analytics SDKs.
  • The Accessibility service only reads the screen and acts while a script you started or scheduled is running, while you record actions, or while you pick a point or crop an image on screen. Screen content is processed on your device; only what you save into a script is kept, and it is never sent to us.
  • When you record typing, every key tap is saved — including in password fields. Anyone who has that script can tell what you typed, so do not share it (see section 1).
  • The “Wait & detect notification” action (if you use it) reads notifications from the one app you choose, only while a script with that action is running, to read the amount aloud. Notifications are not saved or sent anywhere (see section 2).
  • Everything you create (scripts, images, schedules, run history) is kept in the app’s private storage on your phone and is removed when you uninstall the app.
  • Only install AutoGest from Google Play. “Mod” or “cracked” copies on other websites are not made by us and can misuse Accessibility access to steal your data (see section 9).

1. Accessibility service (AccessibilityService API)

AutoGest lets you build or record automation scripts: sequences of taps, swipes, text input and other actions that the app replays for you. To do this it uses Android’s Accessibility service. You must turn the service on yourself in the system Settings, after reading an in-app disclosure that explains what it does. When it is on, the app:

  • Reads the content shown on screen — text, content descriptions and positions of elements in the app in front of you — to find text for a “Click text” step or a “text” condition, and to know where the on-screen keyboard is. While you record typing, it reads the text field you are typing into to recognize what you typed; while a “Type characters” step runs, it reads that field again to check the typing came out right.
  • Takes screenshots (Android 11 and newer) only when you pick a color on screen, freeze the screen to pick a point or crop a template image, or when a script has an image or color step or condition, to compare the screen with the image or color you chose. Screenshots are held in memory only while matching or while you pick on the frozen screen, then discarded; they are never saved to storage. The only part kept is an area you crop yourself as a template image.
  • Performs actions on your behalf — tap, long press, swipe, scroll, type text, tap keys on the on-screen keyboard, press Back, Home, Recent apps and open the apps you picked — exactly as your script’s steps say.
  • Records your touches when you use the recorder, or when you record keys for a “Type characters” step, so they can become script steps. Nothing is added to a script until you review and accept it.
  • Checks whether a new notification is an incoming call, only while a script is running and only if “Stop on incoming call” is on (it is on by default), so the script can stop and leave the phone to you. It looks only at which app posted the notification, its category (for example “call”) and its layout style — never at the caller, the text or anything else in the notification. It also checks whether the phone is ringing. Nothing about calls is stored.

Typing and password fields

When you record real touches and type on the on-screen keyboard, the app saves each key tap: where on the keyboard you tapped, the timing between taps, the size and position of the keyboard and the package name of your keyboard app. It also saves the text it recognized, so you can check it.

In a password field the app does not save the characters, but it still saves every key tap. Replaying those taps types the same password, and anyone who has the script can work out what you typed from them. The review screen and the step card warn you when this happens. Do not record passwords you would not want someone else to have, and do not share or export a script that contains them.

The service does not read the screen, take screenshots or act at any other time: not when no script is running, you are not recording and you are not picking on screen. We do not use the Accessibility service to collect personal or sensitive data, to change your settings behind your back, or for advertising, and we never receive anything it reads. You can turn it off at any time in Settings > Accessibility.

2. Notification access (NotificationListenerService)

This access is only needed for the “Wait & detect notification” action (Pro), which waits for balance alerts from your bank and reads the amount aloud — for example as a payment speaker in your shop. The app only asks for it when you add that action, after a separate in-app disclosure, and you turn it on yourself in Settings > Notification access. If you do not use the action, you do not need it. When it is on:

  • Reading notifications from the app you choose. Android passes every notification to an app with this access, but AutoGest only reads the text (title and body) of notifications from the app you picked for the action (such as your bank or Messages app), and only while a script with that action is running, to find the amount, balance and transfer note. Notifications from other apps, or that arrive when no such script is running, are ignored immediately.
  • Picking a sample. While you pick a sample, the notifications on your phone that contain an amount are shown so you can choose one and tap the amount. The script stores only the app name and the labels in front of the amount, balance and note (such as “PS:”, “SD:”, “ND:”) — never account numbers, amounts, balances, sender names or any other text of the alert.
  • Reading aloud. The amount (and the balance and note, if you turn them on) is spoken by the text-to-speech engine installed on your phone (Google, Samsung…), on the device.

A notification is kept only in the app’s temporary memory until it has been read, then discarded: no notification is written to storage, the run history or logs, and nothing is sent anywhere. You can turn this access off at any time in Settings > Notification access.

5. What we do not collect

The app does not collect, transmit, sell or share any personal data. It has no Internet permission, no user accounts, no advertising, no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs, and does not access your location, contacts, camera or microphone. It does not request permission to read SMS or the call log; the “Wait & detect notification” action only reads notifications from the app you choose, as described in section 2. It does not request the phone state permission: stopping on an incoming call works only from the notification check described in section 1, and never learns who is calling.

6. Sharing a script

A script leaves your phone only when you choose to export or share it. The exported .autoscript file contains the script’s steps — including recorded key taps and text you typed while recording — and any template images it uses. Check a script before you share it, and never share one that contains a password or other private text. You pick where it goes (Downloads folder, a messaging app, email…), and that destination’s own privacy policy applies. Importing a file someone sent you only reads that file. A “Wait & detect notification” action only carries the app name and the sample’s labels, never the text of an alert.

7. Google Play

The app is distributed through Google Play. Depending on your device settings, Google may collect crash and performance reports (Android vitals) and share aggregated statistics with us through the Play Console.

AutoGest Pro is sold as an in-app purchase through Google Play Billing. Payment is handled entirely by Google Play; we never see your payment details. The app only receives from Google Play a signed record of whether your account owns Pro, checks it on your device and keeps the result there (section 3). Pro belongs to your Google account, so reinstalling from Google Play or moving to a new phone with the same account restores it for free.

Google’s processing is covered by Google’s Privacy Policy.

8. Retention and deletion

Data stays on your phone until you delete it. You can delete scripts, schedules and history inside the app, clear everything with Settings > Apps > AutoGest > Storage > Clear data, or uninstall the app. Because we hold no copy of your data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

9. Security

Your data is kept in the app’s private storage, which Android isolates from other apps. The app has no network access, so it cannot leak your data over the Internet.

Only install AutoGest from Google Play. We do not publish the app anywhere else. Copies offered on other websites as “mod”, “cracked” or “Pro unlocked” are not made by us and are not covered by this policy. Because such a copy asks for the same Accessibility access, a malicious one can read everything on your screen — including messages, passwords and banking codes — tap and type on your behalf, and send that data away. If you installed AutoGest from anywhere other than Google Play, turn off its Accessibility service, uninstall it and install it again from Google Play.

10. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

11. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when the app changes. The effective date above will change with each update. If the Accessibility service or the “Wait & detect notification” action starts reading new kinds of data, the app will show the matching disclosure again and ask for your consent before continuing.
